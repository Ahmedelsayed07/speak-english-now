# Voice and Tone

## The core rule
Write the way the trainer talks to one person, not the way a brand talks to an audience.

## Egyptian Arabic, always
- `عايز` not `أريد` · `إزاي` not `كيف` · `دلوقتي` not `الآن` · `مش` not `ليس`
- Second person singular. Never `حضراتكم`.

## Forbidden
- Em dashes (`—`) as a rhetorical device in Arabic prose. Use a full stop.
- `في الحقيقة` · `تجدر الإشارة` · `بلا شك` · `دعنا نتفق`
- Over-formatting personal messages. Lessons and tasks are formatted; thanks,
  apologies, feedback and replies to students are written plainly.
- Promising fluency, speed, or "speaking like a native".

## Structure of a lesson message
```
☀️ صباح الخير — اليوم X
[one-line framing]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[section 1 heading]
[content]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

⚠️ [the trap]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🎧 الفويس تحت
التاسك الساعة ١٢ 🐥
```

## Structure of a voice script
Timestamped blocks. Every block under 50 seconds. Pause markers are mandatory:

```
**[0:00 · 25 seconds]**
> [spoken text]
*(pause 3 seconds)*
```

Total length: 2.5–3 minutes. Never longer.

## The trainer's recurring lines
- «التعلّم بيحصل في دماغك. التدريب بيحصل في بقك.»
- «متحضّرش إجابة — حضّر مادة.»
- «اللي بيستنى لحد ما يبقى جاهز، عمره ما بيبدأ.»
- «الرقم بيغيّر كل حاجة.»

## Handling students
- Public correction: **one** good thing + **one** fix. Never more.
- Negative feedback goes to DM, never to the group.
- A student who admits fear gets the fear acknowledged before any advice.
